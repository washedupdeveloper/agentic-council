# M29 — One place is one finding

The merge key was `(dimension, file, line)`. That is the first thing this
project's foundations page could not defend: nothing checks that a finding's
`dimension` is apt, and the key made the model's label structural.

## What was actually wrong

The first draft of this record claimed a label could *launder* a defect past the
gate. It cannot, quite: a `high` readability finding blocks on severity, and a
`low` correctness finding blocks on dimension. The real defects are smaller and
still worth fixing:

1. **One defect, two rows.** Two samples that agreed a line was wrong but named
   different dimensions produced two findings — two rows in `verdict.json`, two
   SARIF results, two things for a reader to reconcile. The evidence said one
   thing and the artefact said two.
2. **The merged label depended on which row you read.** With two rows, "is this
   blocking?" had two answers, and neither was more authoritative than the other.

## Decision

Merge on the place: `(file, line)`. The merged finding takes

- the **most blocking dimension** any sample used — `security` and `correctness`
  first (they block at any severity), then the canonical order;
- the **highest severity** any sample claimed;
- every claim text and every proof, as before.

The property this buys is monotonicity: if any sample's finding blocks, the
merged finding blocks. A merge can never weaken a defect, and it can never
duplicate one.

## Rejected

- **Keep the dimension in the key and dedupe at report time.** Two sources of
  truth for "how many findings", and the verdict would still be computed over
  rows rather than places.
- **Key on the claim text.** Two samples rarely word a claim identically; the
  place is the only stable identity a model cannot paraphrase away.
- **Trust the label but rank severities.** Leaves the duplicate rows.

## Known trade-offs

- A security issue and a performance issue on the *same line* are one finding.
  Both claim texts and both proofs are kept, but only one dimension label
  survives — the blocking one. The alternative was two rows that a reader has to
  merge by hand.
- The baseline fingerprint includes the dimension, so an accepted finding whose
  merged label changes will not match its old entry. That is a suppression that
  stops working loudly rather than silently.
